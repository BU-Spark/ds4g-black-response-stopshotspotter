## 2. Overview
ShotSpotter is a gunshot detection system provided by the private company SoundThinkingTM. The service claims to enhance the safety of both communities and police officers by deploying large arrays of acoustic sensors in concentrated areas to detect precise location, quantity, and variety of rounds fired.

The continued deployment of ShotSpotter is a concern for the Cambridge-based advocacy group The Black Response. Founded to defund and abolish the Cambridge police, the group advocates on behalf of Black residents, who they feel are poorly represented by the city’s council. In pursuit of these goals, this project involves analyzing data from various sources to determine the effects of ShotSpotter and the surrounding context.

### 2.1 Questions & Hypotheses
We seek to understand how the implementation of namely, ShotSpotter affects housing prices in Cambridge, MA. Our hypothesis is that neighborhoods with more ShotSpotter devices will have higher property values (seen as higher sales prices over time, rental prices over time, etc.), as the technology intends to promote community safety and result in higher policing in the area. This hypothesis is based on client predictions of urban renewals in those zones.

Some caveats to this hypothesis are that ShotSpotter devices are not accurate and are unjustly targeting minorities, changing the demographics of neighborhoods, and ultimately doing more harm than good. We can find evidence of this bias through inequitable distribution of devices throughout the city, understand its effectiveness compared to 911 reports, number of false arrests and unconfirmed incidents, and so on.

### 2.2 Approach
In order to evaluate this hypothesis, we are pursuing two approaches: a comprehensive analysis of the political rhetoric surrounding ShotSpotter and a data analysis study to examine the correlation between ShotSpotter deployments and housing prices.
